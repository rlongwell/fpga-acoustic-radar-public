# Assemble and bring up the three microphones

For a six-microphone build, refer to the [six-microphone wiring table and XDC](six_microphone_breadboard.md) when wiring JE and selecting your FPGA constraints.

**Start with the Zybo completely unpowered.** Switch it off; unplug USB/JTAG, the barrel supply, and any other connection that can supply power. Keep the JE harness disconnected while soldering and checking it. Use only the Zybo's JE 3.3 V for the microphones; never put 5 V on a microphone or FPGA GPIO. Do not plug or unplug microphone branches while powered.

1. **Identify the actual module pads.** Before soldering, photograph both sides of one module straight-on, with all labels and the component area sharp. Use the same mounting notch or an added marker to relate the two views. If boards differ, photograph each revision. Match six pads by label: `VDD/3V/3V3`, `GND`, `SCK/BCLK`, `WS/LRCLK`, `SD/DOUT`, and `L/R/SEL`. Assign MIC1–MIC3 labels before cutting or routing leads. The [logical terminal chart](../wiring/inmp441_breakout_logical_pinout.svg) does not provide physical positions; if silkscreen is ambiguous or absent, stop before soldering a mapped harness or applying power. Do not use package pin numbers.
2. **Inspect the passives and solder the headers.** The loose male pins must be soldered into the corresponding pads for a reliable connection. Confirm the header strip matches the actual hole geometry; split into separate strips/pins if required. Hold the solder tails square in a jig, tack one pin, check seating and module clearance, then solder the rest. Use a magnifier to look for bridges, cold joints and loose pins. Keep the MEMS acoustic port free of flux, solder, tape and mounting adhesive. Do not immerse or blow high-pressure air into the port. Identify any onboard VDD bypass and SD pull-down as described in the [electrical notes](electrical_design_and_i2s.md).
3. **Build the distribution point.** Reserve four electrically separate shared buses on a small breadboard or soldered harness: `+3V3`, `GND`, `I2S_SCK`, `I2S_WS`. Reserve one isolated row each for `MIC1_SD`, `MIC2_SD` and `MIC3_SD`. Check breadboard rails for midpoint gaps and bridge gaps only within a named rail. Avoid putting two pins of a module in a single connected breadboard row accidentally. The supplied female-to-female leads mate with the soldered male module pins and male posts at the distribution end. Use a labeled 2×6 male (2.54 mm) JE adapter or properly fitting male pigtails at the board's female socket. For a JE breakout that presents male posts, use female-to-male leads from those posts into the breadboard; choose male-to-male only where both ends are sockets.
4. **Wire the microphone side.** Follow the [color wiring diagram](../wiring/wiring_three_microphones.svg). Connect each VDD to the red 3.3 V rail, GND to the black return, SCK to the blue clock bus, and WS to the green clock bus. Strap each L/R directly to common ground (gray). Connect only MIC1 SD to the yellow private row, MIC2 SD to the orange row, and MIC3 SD to the purple row. Run grounds alongside clocks and keep the distribution hub close to JE. Fit one 100 kΩ from each SD row to common GND (R1–R3) unless an equivalent is verified onboard. Fit each 100 nF X7R across that module's VDD and GND (C1–C3) with very short connections if local bypass is unconfirmed. A capacitor at the distribution hub does not replace module-local bypass.
5. **Wire the JE adapter while unplugged.** From the Zybo's component side, with the slide switches nearest you, locate the leftmost Pmod on that edge next to the USB host corner; confirm the `JE` markings. Then lower your eye to look straight into the **female** socket with the PCB beneath it; use the [mating-face drawing](../wiring/pmod_je_mating_face.svg). The upper row reads left-to-right **6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1**; the lower row reads **12, 11, 10, 9, 8, 7**. Pin 1 is upper right; the 3.3 V sockets are at the left end. Do not transfer this pattern to a rear view of the mating plug. Meter each adapter contact through to its labeled post. Connect JE6/12 to the +3V3 rail and JE5/11 to the GND rail; connect JE1 to SCK and JE2 to WS. Wire JE3 to MIC1_SD, JE4 to MIC2_SD and JE7 to MIC3_SD. Keep JE8, JE9 and JE10 open. Keep bare metal insulated so adjacent pins cannot touch.
6. **Meter the harness before plugging it in.** With the microphones initially disconnected, check every path from the contacts of the male JE plug to the module-end sockets. Expect near lead resistance for intended wires and L/R-to-GND straps. There must be no low-ohm or continuity path between +3V3 and GND, any two private SD rows, either clock and an SD, or SCK and WS. A charging capacitor may briefly read low; wait for it to settle. With the external pull-downs fitted, each SD-to-GND measures near 100 kΩ when the module is absent; two SD rows can measure about 200 kΩ through ground, which is not a short. Reattach the modules one at a time, inspect each soldered pin-to-lead mapping and recheck for low-ohm shorts. Populated modules can give different resistance readings in either polarity; do not use a resistance or continuity range on a powered board.
7. **Verify the rail before running clocks.** With the harness still unplugged, use a secure adapter or insulated probes to measure JE6 and JE12 relative to JE5/11 at about +3.3 V, once with the Zybo powered on in its normal approved power configuration. Do not bridge adjacent sockets with a probe. Shut down and disconnect the supply again, verify zero voltage, then seat the correctly oriented JE plug. Use a known FPGA configuration where JE clocks are idle or high-impedance and all SD pins are inputs. Prevent an unrelated design from driving JE; disable clocks during power-up. Power the Zybo on and measure about +3.3 V with the **red meter lead on VDD and black on local GND** at each module. Check L/R is at 0 V; stop if any rail is reversed, sags unexpectedly, or a module heats.
8. **Enable clocks only after the rail is present.** This package contains interface constraints and timing documentation, so a separate clock/receiver design or controlled bench clock source is needed for a functional audio check. Do not drive the lines simultaneously from bench and FPGA. Verify JE1/farthest SCK is about 3.072 MHz, JE2/farthest WS about 48 kHz, and there are 32 SCK cycles per WS half and 64 per frame. Use a 10× oscilloscope probe with a short ground connection at the microphone to look for clean threshold crossings and safe voltage excursions. Scope or decode each SD with a common ground, using true I²S framing and ignoring the first 4,096 complete frames after startup. Speak or tap near each microphone in turn to see independent responses on the intended data line. Recheck with all three connected; if the 20 cm setup rings or shifts timing, shorten and dress the leads before changing components.

For added confidence, bring up one module first, then two, then three, powering down before changing connections. After each addition, verify the same rail and clock checks. For a fourth, use the [MIC4 drawing](../wiring/wiring_fourth_microphone.svg); reuse the same power/clock buses, route MIC4 SD alone to JE8, add R4/C4 as needed, and recheck waveforms at full fanout.
